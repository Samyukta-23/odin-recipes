French Fries

Description

These are the universally compatible hardware accessories that everyone insists on stealing from your plate because their personal firewall is apparently down. They are a simple, open-source commodity: sliced potatoes fried to a crisp, yet they suffer from terrible security vulnerabilities when left unattended. No matter how much processing power you dedicate to guarding them, a nearby user will inevitably execute an unauthorized "fry heist" command. They are the perfect side dish, assuming you actually get to eat them.

Ingredients

  • Raw Hardware Data: 4 large Russet potatoes
  • Thermal Processing Fluid: Vegetable or canola oil, for deep frying
  • Default Encryption: Fine sea salt, to taste
  • Optional Patch: ½ teaspoon paprika or garlic powder for extra flavor

Installation Guide

  1. Format the Data: Peel your Russet potatoes and slice them into uniform, stick-shaped hardware assets, then soak them in cold water for 30 minutes to purge excess starch data.
  2. Initial Thermal Boot: Drain and dry the potatoes completely, then submerge them in oil heated to a low thermal setting of 300°F (150°C) for about 5 to 6 minutes until cooked but pale.
  3. Cooling Cycle: Remove the fries and let them rest on paper towels until they return to room temperature.
  4. Final High-Heat Deploy: Crank the oil temperature up to 400°F (200°C) and flash-fry the assets for 2 to 3 minutes until a golden, crispy outer firewall forms.
  5. Apply Encryption: Toss the hot fries immediately in sea salt and optional spices while they are fresh out of the fryer.
