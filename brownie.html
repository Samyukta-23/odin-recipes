Description

Meet the ultimate legacy code of desserts—dense, heavy, and so structurally unstable that it completely crashes your diet's operating system. This is an over-engineered brick of sugar and cocoa that refuses to refactor for your health goals. It is packed with unauthorized features (mostly chocolate chips) that cause immediate cognitive lag and total system bliss. Consider it a mandatory software update for your soul, even if your fitness tracker flags it as malware.

Ingredients

  • System Core: 1 cup (226g) unsalted butter, melted
  • Primary Code: 2 cups (400g) granulated sugar
  • Dark Mode Graphics: ¾ cup (75g) unsweetened cocoa powder
  • Runtime Drivers: 3 large eggs, room temperature
  • Extension Pack: 1 tablespoon vanilla extract
  • Error Correction: 1 teaspoon salt
  • Mainframe Binder: 1 cup (120g) all-purpose flour
  • Unauthorized Add-ons: 1½ cups (270g) semisweet chocolate chips

Installation Guide

  1. Pre-heat the System: Fire up your oven mainframe to 350°F (175°C) and secure your 8x8-inch hardware pan with grease and parchment paper.
  2. Initialize Liquid Core: Melt the butter in a microwave-safe container until the solid-state blocks are completely converted to liquid.
  3. Overclock the Cocoa: Whisk the sugar and cocoa powder into the hot butter aggressively for 30 seconds to force the shiny, crackly UI top to compile later.
  4. Load the Drivers: Beat in the eggs, vanilla, and salt until the batter achieves a smooth, glossy runtime environment.
  5. Deploy the Hardware: Fold in the flour and chocolate chips with a spatula just until the dry files are integrated—do not over-allocate processing power or overmix.
  6. Execution Phase: Pour the asset into the pan and bake for 35 minutes until a toothpick inserted into the center returns with a few moist crumbs (partial data leakage is normal for fudgy builds).
